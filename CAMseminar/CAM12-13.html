Weizhang Huang

Title: The cutoff method for the numerical computation of nonnegative solutions of parabolic PDEs

 

Abstract: The cutoff method,  which cuts off the values of a function less than a given number, is studied for the numerical computation of nonnegative solutions of parabolic partial differential equations. A convergence analysis is given for a broad class of finite difference methods combined with cutoff for linear parabolic equations. Two applications are investigated, linear anisotropic diffusion problems satisfying the setting of the convergence analysis and nonlinear lubrication-type equations

for which it is unclear if the convergence analysis applies. The numerical results are shown to be consistent with the theory and in good agreement with existing results in the literature. The convergence analysis and applications demonstrate that the cutoff method is an effective tool for use in the computation of nonnegative solutions. Cutoff can also be used with other discretization methods such as collocation, finite volume, finite element, and spectral methods and for the computation of positive solutions.

Weishi Liu

Title: Special static alignments of nematic liquid-crystals

 

Abstract: In this talk, we will first review the variational formulation in the static theory of Oseen-Zocher-Frank-Ericksen for liquid-crystals. For special situations, the associated Euler-Lagrange equations will be examined from the dynamical system point of view; in particular, minimality of critical points of the variational functional will be discussed. The underlying mathematical analysis for liquid-crystal-display (LCD) technology  that was first conducted by F. Leslie will be presented in a much simpler way.

Sept 19

Mingji Zhang

Title: Asymptotic expansions and numerical simulations of I-V relations via Poisson-Nernst-Planck system

 

Abstract:  We investigate higher order matched asymptotic expansions of a steady-state Poisson-Nernst-Planck (PNP) system with particular attention on the I-V relation of ion channels. Numerical simulations are performed to study the boundary value effects on the I-V relations, and we also study the permanent charge effect on the I-V relation. Assuming the Debye length is small, the PNP system can be viewed as a singularly perturbed system. Special structures of the zeroth order inner and outer systems make it possible for more explicit derivation of higher order terms in the asymptotic expansions. For the case of zero permanent charge, our result concerning the I-V relation for two oppositely charged ion species is that the third order corrections to the zeroth order linear I-V relation is cubic when electro-neutrality condition is taken at both ends of the channel, and furthermore, up to the third order, for L>R, the cubic I-V relation has three distinct real roots that corresponds to the bi-stable structure in the Fitzhugh-Nagumo simplification of the Hodgkin-Huxley model. 

Sept 26

Mat Johnson

Title: Stability of Periodic Waves in KdV type Equations with Fractional Dispersion

 

Abstract: When modeling unidirectional propagation of surface water waves of small amplitude and long wavelength, a commonly used approximation of the full water wave equations (a HORRIBLY nonlinear and nonlocal equation) is the Korteweg-de Vries equation, a scalar nonlinear local dispersive equation.  However, there are many other interesting physical phenomena which give rise to KdV type equations where the "dispersive" operator becomes nonlocal; an example is in the modeling of internal waves in deep water, in which case a commonly used model equation is the Benjamin-Ono equation.  While the stability theory is by now well known for such equations in the context of square integrable traveling  wave solutions (the solitary waves), current results in the case where the profile of the traveling wave is spatially periodic seem to be very few, and those that do exist are limited to perturbations which respect the periodic structure of the underlying wave.

 

In this talk, I will discuss new results in the periodic stability theory of such nonlocal KdV type equations.  In particular, we will discuss 1) the nonlinear stability of periodic "constrained energy minimizers" in such equations, which relies on a nonlocal Sturm-Liouville type theory recently developed by Frank and Lenzmann, and 2) the spectral stability of small amplitude periodic waves when subject to arbitrary localized perturbations, a context in which there are seemingly no rigorous results, presumably due to the lack of a Floquet like theory.  The results on topic 1) are joint work with Vera Mikyoung Hur from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign.

Milena Stanislavova

Title: Linear stability for subsonic waves in two water wave models

 

Abstract: In this talk I will apply two different abstract results to study the stability of subsonic traveling waves in the Benney-Luke model and in the Boussinesq 'abc'-system. Both models are approximations of the full water wave equation, which describe small amplitude long waves in water of finite length. For the Benney-Luke equation, a threshold speed that separates the stable and unstable waves will be computed, while indices counting formula will be used for the 'abc'-system. In both cases the result will be a complete characterization of stability and instability regions in terms of the parameters and the wave speeds.

Dave Mechem (KU Geography)

Title: Cloud processes in turbulent boundary-layer flows

 

Abstract: Global climate model projections are highly sensitive to the treatment of low-altitude clouds, which are important because of their role in cooling the climate system. Low clouds are a multi-phase system composed of soluble aerosol particles and liquid water drops over a wide range of sizes, all embedded in a turbulent boundary-layer flow. These cloud systems may be viewed in the framework of aerosol–cloud–precipitation interactions, mediated through microphysical, dynamic, and radiative processes. One approach to studying boundary-layer cloud processes is through large-eddy simulation (LES), which resolves the bulk of the turbulent transports. I will briefly introduce the LES technique and discuss some of our research on cloud-topped boundary layers.

Oct 31

Hongguo Xu

Title: Convergence of Wilkinson's shift QR algorithm for normal matrices

 

Abstract: The QR algorithm with Wilkinson's shift is a practical eigenvalue method programed in most numerical linear algebra software. When it is applied to Hermitian matrices, it is known that the algorithm always converges and the convergence rate is at least quadratic. It has been an open problem for 60 years whether the rate is exactly quadratic in some cases, and whether the rate is normally cubic. In this talk, we consider normal matrices. We give an almost complete characterization of the convergence behaviors, and show their relations with eigenvalue locations and the initial Hessenberg reduction vector.

Nov 7

Lennard Kamenski (TU Darmstadt)

Title: How a non-convergent Hessian recovery works in mesh adaptation

 

Abstract: Hessian recovery is widely used in mesh adaptation for obtaining the required directional information for anisotropic mesh adjustment. Unfortunately, Hessian recovery from a linear finite element solution is neither convergent nor even accurate in general. On the other hand, practical experience show that meshes based on such a non-convergent Hessian recovery nevertheless still lead to the optimal decrease rate of the finite element error. In this paper we show that an accurate Hessian recovery is not required for the purpose of mesh adaptation and that a non-convergent Hessian approximation will still lead to a quasi-optimal mesh if it satisfies some relaxed closeness condition. Both situations with positive definite Hessian and general Hessian are studied.

Rod Edwards (University of Victoria)

Title: Singular solutions in gene network models: Filippov or singular perturbation?

 

Abstract: Gene networks involve interactions between genes (or, more correctly, their protein products) that are more or less sharply switching at particular thresholds. Analysis of qualitative behaviour has typically been facilitated by using step functions as the infinitely steep limits of the switching functions. This leads to its own mathematical problems, however, because of the discontinuities in the resulting vector fields. As long as trajectories pass through these discontinuity hyperplanes transversally, no difficulties arise, but trajectories may become confined to them, corresponding to situations where gene product concentrations are kept at threshold values. Two approaches have been proposed for dealing with dynamics in these 'switching domains'. The Filippov method was borrowed from control theory and engineering, where differential inclusions are used and solutions become set valued in general. The singular perturbation approach considers a solution to the step function system as a limit of solutions of smooth systems with steep sigmoidal interactions. These approaches give different solutions in some cases, and much confusion has arisen as to the interpretation of the multiple Filippov solutions, some of which may be spurious. We clarify the relationship between these approaches, making use of an alternate definition of solution from Filippov's original work, and finally show that a more general concept of solution is needed to cover all cases. It turns out that the nonuniqueness cannot entirely be avoided in the case of the step function limit, and corresponds to extreme sensitivity in the steep sigmoidal systems.

Zhijian Wang (KU Aerospace Engineering)

Title: A Unifying Discontinuous Formulation for the Navier-Stokes Equations on Mixed Grids

 

Abstract: A current breakthrough in computational fluid dynamics (CFD) is the emergence of adaptive high-order (order > 2) methods. The leader is the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method, amongst several other methods including the multi-domain spectral, spectral volume (SV), spectral difference (SD) methods. Recently, the correction procedure via reconstruction (CPR) formulation was developed to unify all the methods under a single framework. All these methods possess the following properties: k-exactness on arbitrary grids, and compactness, which is especially important for parallel computing on clusters of CPUs and GPUs. In this talk, I will describe the CPR formulation, and explain its connection to the DG, SV and SD methods. In addition, the application of high-order methods to compute transitional flow over a SD7003 wing and flow over flapping wings will be presented. The talk will conclude with several remaining challenges in the research on high-order methods.

Hyea Hyun Kim (Kyung Hee University, Korea)

Title: A staggered discontinuous Galerkin method for the Stokes system and its fast solvers

 

Abstract: In this talk, we will present a new staggered discontinuous Galerkin method for the Stokes system. The key feature of our method is that the discrete system preserves the structures of the continuous problem, which results from the use of our new staggered DG spaces. This also provides local and global conservation properties, which are desirable for fluid flow applications. The method is based on the first order mixed formulation involving pressure, velocity and velocity gradient. The velocity and velocity gradient are approximated by polynomials of the same degree while the choice of degree for pressure is flexible, namely the approximation degree for the pressure can be chosen as either that of velocity or one degree lower than that of velocity. In any case, stability and optimal convergence of the method are proved. Moreover, a superconvergence result with respect to a discrete H1-norm for the velocity is proved. In addition, we present a domain decomposition algorithm for fast solution of our DG formulation.

Atanas Stefanov

Title: Hamiltonian-Krein instability index and applications

 

Abstract: The Hamiltonian-Krein (instability) index is concerned with determining the number of unstable eigenvalues for an operator in the form  $J L$, where $J$ is skew-symmetric and $L$ is self-adjoint. If $J$ has a bounded inverse, the theory is well-developed. There is however an important class of problems, for which $J$ fails to have a bounded inverse. We overcome this difficulty by considering an equivalent eigenvalue problem, where $J$ does have a bounded inverse, namely the Hilbert transform. We use the index to characterize the spectral stability of homoclinic traveling waves for fractional KdV and BBM type problems.

Vera Mikyoung Hur (University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign)

Title: Surface water waves: gain of regularity vs. blowup

 

Abstract: I will speak on the initial value problem for surface waves on a two-dimensional infinitely-deep flow of water, under the influence of gravity and surface tension. I will begin by giving a precise account of the formulation of the surface water wave problem and discuss its reformulation as a system of nonlinear dispersive equations. I will illustrate formation of singularity in finite time for a model equation of gravity waves. I will then describe instantaneous, gain of high regularities due to the effects of surface tension for the full problem.

George Tsoflias (KU Geology)

Title: Geophysical Monitoring of the Subsurface: From Fluid-Flow in Fractures to Ice Streams

 

Abstract: Radar methods developed at the University of Kansas are providing insights to problems spanning a broad range of scales, from predicting the fluid-flow properties of fractures to understanding the behavior of rapidly changing ice streams. Advances in the understanding of the response of ground penetrating radar (GPR) signal (amplitude and phase as a function of frequency, angle of incidence and polarization) to fracture properties (fracture aperture, fluid content and salinity) allow us to relate geophysical observations to fractured rock hydrologic properties. Saline tracer tests monitored by GPR revealed flow channels trending across the survey area. GPR amplitude "breakthrough" provided estimates of mass transport along the fracture that are in good agreement to estimates derived from chemical monitoring mass breakthrough in boreholes. This work shows that GPR imaging offers the capability to define the geometry of flow channeling through sub-millimeter aperture fractures and reduce the uncertainty of transport predictive modeling in groundwater systems. Unprecedented changes have occurred in recent years at outlet glaciers with significant implications to sea level change predictions. Analysis of airborne radar data acquired by the Center for Remote Sensing of Ice Sheets (CReSIS) at Jakobshavn Glacier, Greenland, offers new insights to past climate variability, ice dynamics and accumulation rate patterns hundreds of kilometers inland from its terminus over a region commonly overlooked in glaciological studies.

Jingyue Wang

Title: Anisotropic mesh adaptation for eigenvalue problems

 

Abstract: Many modern problems in physics or engineering involve solving eigenvalue problems for partial differential equations. It is well known that the finite element method with uniform meshes for PDEs gives good approximations but also leads to a very high computational effort. It has been an important research topic in the last decades to use adaptive meshes to reduce the computational complexity, where the meshes applied are usually isotropic. In this talk, we discuss anisotropic mesh adaptation for finite element approximation of eigenvalue problems. Anisotropic meshes are more appropriate for problems with anisotropic features, i.e., anisotropic diffusions, etc. We give the numerical analysis for our approach. Preliminary numerical results will be discussed.

Aijun Zhang

Title: Competing Interactions and Traveling Wave Solutions in Lattice Differential Equations

 

Abstract: The existence of traveling front solutions to bistable lattice differential equations in the absence of a comparison principle is studied. The results are in the spirit of those in Bates, Chen, and Chmaj for TRAVELING WAVES OF BISTABLE DYNAMICS ON A LATTICE, but are applicable to vector equations and to more general limiting systems. An abstract result on the persistence of traveling wave solutions is obtained and is then applied to lattice differential equations with repelling first and/or second neighbor interactions.

Kelly McQuighan (Brown Univerisity)

Title: Oscillons near Hopf Bifurcations of Periodically Forced Planar Reaction Diffusion Equations

 

Abstract: Oscillons are planar, spatially localized, temporally oscillating, radially symmetric structures. They have been observed in various experimental contexts, including fluid systems, granular systems, and chemical systems. Oscillons often arise near forced Hopf bifurcations, which are modeled mathematically with the forced complex Ginzburg-Landau (FCGL) equation. We present a proof of the existence of oscillons in the forced planar complex Ginzburg-Landau equation through a geometric blow-up analysis. Our analysis is complemented by a numerical continuation study of oscillons in the forced Ginzburg-Landau equation using Matlab and AUTO.

Milena Stanislavova

Title: Linear stability analysis for special solutions of second order in time PDE - the higher dimensional case

 

Abstract: We develop a general theory to treat the linear stability of certain special solutions of second order in time evolutionary PDE. We apply these results to standing waves of the following problems: the Klein-Gordon equation, for which we consider both ground states and certain excited states, the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov system and the beam equation. We also discuss possible applications to some non-standard ground and excited states for the Klein-Gordon model as well as multidimensional traveling waves (not necessarily homoclinic to zero) for general second order equations of this type. In all cases, our abstract results provide a complete characterization of the linear stability of such solutions.

Hongguo Xu

Title: Computing orthogonal eigenvalues with CS decomposition

 

Abstract: We consider the problem of computing the eigenvalues of a real orthogonal matrix. We show that under some conditions a real orthogonal matrix Q is orthogonally similar to a product DZDZ^T, where D is a special signature matrix and Z is a 2-by-2 block matrix with all four blocks being bidiagonal. We explain how a Schur form of Q can be determined by a full CS decomposition of Z. Some numerical examples are provided.

 

This is the joint work with Daniela Calvetti and Lothar Reichel.

May 1

Volker Mehrmann (TU Berlin)

Title: Adaptive Finite Element Methods and Adaptive Linear Algebra for Non-Selfadjoint PDE Eigenvalue Problems

 

Abstract: This talk presents  adaptive algorithms for eigenvalue problems associated with non-selfadjoint partial differential operators. The basis for the developed algorithms is a homotopy method which departs from a well-understood selfadjoint problem. Apart from the adaptive grid refinement, the progress of the homotopy as well as the solution of the iterative method are adapted to balance the contributions of the different error sources. The first algorithm balances the homotopy, discretization and approximation errors with respect to a fixed stepsize  in the homotopy. The second algorithm combines the adaptive stepsize control for the homotopy with an adaptation in space that ensures an error below a fixed tolerance. The outcome of this paper leads to the third algorithm which allows the complete adaptivity in space, homotopy stepsize as well as the iterative algebraic eigenvalue solver. All three algorithms are compared in numerical examples.

Xuemin Tu

Title: Implicit sampling for uncertainty quantification of an elliptic inverse problem

 

Abstract:  There are increasing interest in uncertainty quantification for differential equations with uncertain input data. These data can be the initial conditions, boundary conditions, or the parameters in the differential equations. In this talk, we focus on the uncertainty quantification of an elliptic inverse problem, where the uncertain data is the diffusion coefficient. The Bayesian approach is used and the implicit sampling method is used to sample the posterior density.
